# Welo · Absenteeism Analytics Mockup

A presentation dashboard for Welo's absenteeism intelligence, **wired to the real
absenteeism model output**. Every headline number, risk band, fatigue segment,
cohort breakdown and individual prediction on the page is generated by the model
in [`model/`](model/), not hardcoded.

Open [`index.html`](index.html) in a browser. It hydrates itself from
`model/data/outputs/dashboard_feed.js`. If the feed is missing it still renders
with the designed fallback numbers, so it never breaks for a screenshot.

## Industry versions

The same dashboard engine serves several industry framings, selected by a query
param so there is only one file to maintain:

| URL | Version |
| --- | --- |
| `index.html` | Industrial, sector neutral (default) |
| `index.html?industry=mining` | Mining |
| `index.html?industry=manufacturing` | Manufacturing |
| `index.html?industry=logistics` | Logistics |
| `index.html?industry=generic` | Cross-industry, no sector wording |

**The default is the sector-neutral industrial version**, and that is the one
that goes to prospective buyers. It is blue collar wording that fits any
employer with a shift-based frontline workforce: plants, a distribution centre,
a depot, and absence that has to be covered on the day. A demo framed as one
vertical asks every other buyer to do the translating, so the verticals are
profiles rather than the baseline. The markup defaults carry the neutral wording
too, so a failed config load degrades to neutral rather than to a vertical.

Each version also carries its own accent colour (industrial and mining keep the
Welo brand red, generic teal, manufacturing steel-blue, logistics indigo) so the
pages are visibly distinct. The recolour only touches the brand ramp; risk-band
colours stay constant for consistent semantics.

**Manufacturing and logistics have their own sector-calibrated data**, not just
different copy. Each loads its own `dashboard_feed.<sector>.js` generated from a
synthetic cohort whose input distributions are calibrated to that sector's
workforce norms (see below). The `industrial`, `mining` and `generic` profiles
share the base feed, which is the one the model was actually fitted on.
Industry-variable copy is tagged with `data-ind` in `index.html` and supplied
per profile in [`config/industry.js`](config/industry.js).

The result: the base industrial cohort runs ~5.3% modelled absence,
manufacturing ~3.6% (lighter, more stable), logistics ~7.9% (long hours, worse
sleep, driver-fatigue) - the same model scoring three different populations.

### Currency

Amounts are stored in Rand and converted on the fly. A **ZAR / USD** toggle in
the header switches every figure (headline, cohort, individual, HR, ROI) at once;
`?currency=usd` sets it from a link. Both dashboards have it: the standalone one
and the platform shell.

The rate carries the date it was taken, and that date is shown beside the
figures whenever dollars are on screen. Set the two together or neither is
used, because a current rate next to a stale date is how a converted figure
ends up carrying somebody else's date.

| | Standalone dashboard | Platform shell |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set the rate | [`config/industry.js`](config/industry.js), `WELO_FX = { zarPerUsd, asOf }` | `WELO_FX_ZAR_PER_USD` and `WELO_FX_AS_OF` |
| Formatter | `money()` in `index.html` | [`lib/platform/currency.js`](lib/platform/currency.js) |

In the platform, money renders through the `<Money zar={...} />` leaf rather
than a string helper, so a server-rendered screen converts without becoming a
client component. There is deliberately no Rand formatter left in
`modules/sick-leave/format.js`: a screen that builds its own `"R" + number`
renders a figure the toggle cannot reach, and CI fails on one.

The agents are not given the rate. They reason in Rand, the stored unit, and
the panel says so beside the answer when dollars are on screen, rather than
asking a model to do arithmetic whose result would be stated in confident
prose.

### Switching versions

Client links carry no visible switcher. To flip between versions yourself (for
previews or live in a meeting), add `&preview=1` (or `?preview=1`) to the URL,
e.g. `index.html?industry=generic&preview=1`. A small industry dropdown then
appears in the header; it never shows on the plain client links.

## What the dashboard shows

The dashboard is organised around **cohorts**, not sites. The model's signal is
operational and clinical (fatigue/burnout, smoking + alcohol load, metabolic
load, workload), so the workforce is grouped by the levers Welo can actually act
on rather than by geography.

- **Portfolio** - model-level headline: covered lives, predicted absent days
  (90d), cost exposure, addressable saving, risk distribution, fatigue/burnout
  segment, driver concentration, and a cohort summary table.
- **Cohorts** - group the covered workforce by **operational load**, **fatigue
  band**, **age**, **tenure** or **lifestyle risk**. Each cohort card shows its
  high/critical concentration, days per head, mean fatigue, cost exposure and
  addressable saving. Click a cohort to drill in.
- **Individuals** - the model scores every covered life. High and critical
  employees are listed first. Filter by risk band or cohort, sort, search, and
  click any row to open a profile drawer with the full prediction: risk-band
  probabilities, fatigue score, the drivers behind the prediction (SHAP where
  available, otherwise the model features sitting above the cohort norm), the
  employee's profile, and their cohort memberships.
- **HR & Ops** - the operational view for the HR team: predicted absence
  rate, shift cover-gap days and overtime backfill cost, absence frequency
  (Bradford-style), return-to-work caseload and leave hygiene, broken down by
  operational cohort, plus an HR action playbook. Cost and spell assumptions
  are benchmark-anchored and documented in the feed's `hr_ops.assumptions`.
- **Outcomes & ROI** - projected addressable saving, seasonality and an
  intervention scenario.

> The individual records are synthetic industrial workforce rows, not real
> people, and no client data was ever used to produce them. Every synthetic row
> is tagged `synthetic_industrial_v1__sa_shift_workforce` so it can be swapped
> for real client data without touching the dashboard. On the calibration
> anchor, precisely: the input distributions are anchored to published South
> African mining-cohort epidemiology, because that is the best-documented
> published source for a South African frontline workforce on a shift roster.
> The demo presents it as an industrial workforce, which is a reasonable stand-in
> across heavy industry, and the model card says what the anchor is rather than
> implying a broader evidence base than exists.

### Why cohorts instead of sites

Three reasons, open to challenge:

1. **The model has no site signal.** It predicts from health, fatigue and
   workload features. Cohorts defined on those features line up with where the
   absence actually concentrates, e.g. the *smoker + drinker* cohort runs a
   33% high/critical share vs 3% for *low lifestyle risk*; *high-intensity ops*
   runs 22% vs 6% for *light duty*. Site-level cuts wash that out.
2. **Cohorts map to interventions.** A high-fatigue or high-overtime cohort has
   an obvious rostering/clinical response; "Site 2" does not.
3. **Site data may not exist on day one.** Cohorts are derivable from the feed
   the model already produces, so the view works before any HR/location join.

When real site/department fields arrive they become just another cohort lens;
the Cohorts screen already supports multiple dimensions.

## AI agents (Anthropic API)

The dashboard can host three genuinely AI-powered agents that reason over the
model output: an **Analyst** (portfolio questions), a **Case Assistant** (drafts
a support and return-to-work plan for an individual), and a **Cover
Coordinator** (turns cover-gap and overtime into rostering actions). They are
powered by the Anthropic Messages API.

The API key must stay server-side, so the agents run inside the existing
`welo_inference` FastAPI service (`ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`), and the dashboard calls
that proxy over HTTP through [`config/agents.js`](config/agents.js). Point a
link at a deployed proxy with `index.html?api=https://<service-url>`. With no
proxy configured the dashboard falls back to built-in, non-AI summaries, so the
shareable static build never breaks.

The Case Assistant is wired into the Individual profile screen as the reference
integration. Full architecture, environment variables, endpoints and cost are
in [`model/welo_inference/AGENTS.md`](model/welo_inference/AGENTS.md).

## The model (`model/`)

`model/` is the full absenteeism stack: a config-driven training pipeline
(`welo_pipeline`), a FastAPI inference service (`welo_inference`), trained
artifacts (`models/`), and the cached dashboard feed (`data/outputs/`). See
[`model/MODEL_README.md`](model/MODEL_README.md) for the pipeline, the inference
service, and the Cloud Run deployment.

Model performance (cross-validated, from `model/reports/model_metrics.json`):
regression CV R² ≈ 0.40 / MAE ≈ 6.0 hours; classification CV ROC-AUC (macro OvR)
≈ 0.84.

## Regenerating the dashboard feed

The base feed (headline, risk distribution, fatigue, intervention queue with
SHAP reasons, global importance, metrics) comes from the training pipeline:

```bash
cd model
python -m welo_pipeline --config configs/demo.yaml
```

The dashboard additionally needs **cohort aggregates** and **per-individual
records**. Those are added by `model/build_cohort_feed.py`, which reproduces the
deterministic synthetic cohort (seed 7, n=5800, validated to match the
predictions 1:1 on actual absence hours), joins it to `predictions.csv`, derives
the cohort dimensions, and writes the enriched `dashboard_feed.json` / `.js`:

```bash
cd model
pip install numpy pandas pyyaml
python build_cohort_feed.py
```

The **sector feeds** (manufacturing, logistics) are generated by
`model/build_sector_feeds.py`. It builds a sector-calibrated synthetic cohort
(`model/sector_cohorts.py`), scores it with the **same persisted model** as the
base cohort (one model, different populations: only the input distributions
change),
and runs the shared enrichment (`model/feed_enrich.py`) to write
`dashboard_feed.manufacturing.js` and `dashboard_feed.logistics.js`. Global
feature importance and metrics are reused from the trained model; SHAP per-row
reasons are omitted for the sector cohorts, so the dashboard shows indicative
drivers there.

```bash
cd model
pip install numpy pandas pyyaml scikit-learn joblib
python build_sector_feeds.py
```

The sector calibrations in `sector_cohorts.py` are illustrative, anchored to
plausible SA sector norms, and stay illustrative until real client data lands.

The two illustrative benchmark panels are also sector-specific: the clinical
heatmaps (driver concentration, chronic-disease hotspots) and the seasonality
panels (52-week calendar, quarterly bars, addressable-saving-by-driver) are
re-skinned per sector from the `benchmarks` block in
[`config/industry.js`](config/industry.js): e.g. manufacturing leads on
musculoskeletal + metabolic with a post-festive production ramp, logistics on
metabolic + sleep/fatigue with a festive peak-season spike. Industrial, mining
and generic keep the static markup. These tables are illustrative benchmarks, not model
output.

The chronic-disease hotspots panel also exists in the platform shell, on the
absence Portfolio screen, carrying the dashboard's shipped-default figures
byte for byte so the two surfaces cannot quote different numbers at the same
prospect. Its data and its column control live in
[`modules/absence/data/chronic.js`](modules/absence/data/chronic.js), kept out
of the generated `portfolio.js` so a hand-written benchmark and pipeline
output never share one object. The platform version adds one control the
standalone does not have: columns are derived from the live cohort list, and a
cohort under the suppression threshold is dropped rather than greyed, because
a greyed column still puts a health prevalence beside the name of a group too
small to report on. The driver-concentration heatmap has not been ported.

Then reopen `index.html`.
